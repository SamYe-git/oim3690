OIM3690 Lightning Talk

Your next website visitor might not be a human.

What OpenAI's "Businesses in ChatGPT" means for people who build websites.

Sam Ye · November 2026

Hi everyone. Today I want to talk about something OpenAI launched nine days ago, and why I think it matters for all of us who have spent this semester building websites.

Quick poll

When you want to buy something, do you still Google it, or do you ask ChatGPT?

1.2B people use ChatGPT every week

Source: OpenAI, "Introducing Businesses in ChatGPT," Nov 10, 2026

Raise your hand if you've asked ChatGPT for a product recommendation in the last month. (Pause.) That's the shift. OpenAI says 1.2 billion people use ChatGPT each week, and businesses want to be part of those conversations.

What launched · Nov 10, 2026 · Beta

Businesses in ChatGPT has two parts

Business Hub

Claim and verify your business profile.

Upload product feeds with price and availability.

See aggregated mentions, views, and website clicks from ChatGPT.

Global, where ChatGPT is available

Business Agents

A brand's own AI agent that answers customer questions inside ChatGPT.

Clearly labeled and kept separate from your main chat.

US e-commerce & online services only

Both free at launch. Shopify merchants can connect through Business Hub or the Shopify admin.

There are two pieces. Business Hub is the management layer: a business claims its profile, verifies it, and keeps its info and product feeds up to date. It also shows how often the business shows up in ChatGPT. Business Agents go a step further: the brand gets its own AI rep that customers can chat with inside ChatGPT. Right now agents are limited to eligible US e-commerce and online service businesses.

The shift

The customer journey is getting a new front door

Before Google search→ Your website→ Checkout
Now Ask ChatGPT→ Brand agent→ Checkout

Your website goes from being the destination to being the data source.

Here's the part I think matters for us. The old path was: search, land on the website, buy. In the new path, the customer might never visit the website at all. ChatGPT reads the business's data and the agent does the talking. So the website stops being the place people go, and becomes the place AI learns from.

Why this matters for us

From SEO to GEO

  • SEO asked: can Google rank my page?
  • GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) asks: can an AI understand and describe my business correctly?
  • Clean, structured data matters more than pretty layouts here.
  • Business Hub even shows which topics your site appears in.

For web developers this changes the job a little. SEO was about getting Google to rank your page. People now talk about GEO: making sure AI can understand and describe your business correctly. A big part of that is structured data like this JSON-LD block. It's invisible to visitors, but it tells machines exactly what the product is, what it costs, and whether it's in stock. It's the same idea as the product feeds in Business Hub.

A real example

My family's business sells a Chinese herbal supplement

A year ago, reaching US customers meant a Shopify site plus Google ads. Today I'd add one more question:

"How does ChatGPT describe our product, and do we control that answer?"

To make this concrete: my family runs a business selling Tiepi Fengdou, a Chinese herbal supplement. If we wanted to sell in the US, the playbook used to be a good website and Google ads. Now I'd also want to know what ChatGPT says when someone asks about dendrobium supplements, and whether we have any control over that answer. Business Hub is OpenAI's first real answer to that question.

The catches

It's early, and it raises questions

Limited

It's a beta. Agents are US-only and only for eligible e-commerce and online service businesses.

Privacy

OpenAI says businesses don't see your chats. Insights are aggregated and only appear above a usage threshold.

Ads

Businesses can sponsor their agent as a ChatGPT ad destination. Labeled, but is the answer still neutral?

A few catches. First, it's a beta and the agents are narrow for now. Second, privacy: OpenAI says businesses can't read your conversations, they only get aggregated numbers. Third, and this is the interesting one: businesses can pay to sponsor their agent as the destination for ChatGPT ads. Those are labeled, but it raises a real question about whether AI answers stay neutral once money is involved.

One question to leave you with

We spent this semester building websites for humans. Should we start building them for AI too?
